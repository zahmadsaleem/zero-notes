Reading the tide

At low tide, the path around the headland reappears. It’s narrow enough that two people walking in opposite directions have to take turns on the stepping stones.

The tide table gives you a starting point. Wind can push water into the bay, and the level on the rocks can differ from the level at the harbor gauge. Check the water on your way out, and keep an eye on it as you walk.

On the way back

Leave enough time to return by the same route. A quiet cove can fill quickly after the turn, especially where the shore pinches between two ridges.

The tide table gives you a starting point. A line of wet seaweed gives you another. When the lower stones disappear, use the path above the beach.
